One idea. One stop. No more run-ons.

A run-on happens when two complete ideas crash together without a stop. In this lab you learn to find the crash, choose a fix, and write sentences a reader can breathe through.

🩺 Run-On Clinic

The four fixes

Spanish bridge: In Spanish, one long sentence with many commas can be fine. In English, every complete idea needs its own stop: a period, a semicolon, a comma + FANBOYS, or a subordinator.

① Spot the Seam

Tap the word where the SECOND complete idea begins.

② Fix It

Choose a tool. Some sentences accept more than one fix — pick the one that shows the right relationship.

③ Paragraph Checker

Paste or type your own paragraph. The checker underlines sentences that LOOK like run-ons. It is a smart guess, not a judge — read each one aloud and decide.

One-breath test: read each sentence out loud. If you must take a breath in the middle and there is no comma + joining word, no semicolon, and no subordinator, it is probably a run-on.

🧩 Combine-It Studio

Two short sentences. Write ONE sentence that joins them correctly. Use a comma + FANBOYS, a semicolon, or a subordinator. No commas alone!
